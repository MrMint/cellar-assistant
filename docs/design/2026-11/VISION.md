# Vision: your taste, kept

Confirmed by the owner on 2026-11-23 ("Yes, that's it"). Every direction, kit decision and board
in this pass answers to this page. It replaces "a candlelit cellar, a sommelier's notebook, and a
little magic" as the lead idea; that brief's palette, type and motion remain evidence.

## What the app is

A record of everything you have tried and what you thought of it.

The cellar is one place things come from. A bar, a coffee shop and a friend's fridge are others.

## Where it came from

The owner, on their own use:

> a lot of my use was around obviously rating beers and other types of drinks and keeping track
> of what I like and dislike and the tier rankings and everything. I do think the inventories and
> like seller management is super helpful and probably something we continue to make easy.

> my primary use case really being like pulling up the app when I'm like at coffee shops to rate
> places. And then for rating beers at home.

## The moment to make great

Rating something in a few seconds, wherever you are, whether or not you own it or can photograph
it. The owner: "I probably lean towards rating things".

## The payoff

Your rankings and tier lists, which get better every time you rate.

## The feeling

"I know what I like." Opinionated and personal.

## Never

- An inventory system you have to keep up.
- A social feed. Friends are there to compare taste with, not to scroll.
- A pile of features to browse.
- A screen that looks as if nobody chose how it should look.

## What follows from it

- **Home** is what the owner had before: search, shortcuts, and their own recent ratings, with
  rating something new one tap away. ("I think it's a mistake to shift it's focus. I think it
  should be geared around what I had before")
- **Image first.** ("I really liked the image first approach I was taking before.")
- **One way to draw each thing, chosen on purpose.** The core dislike of the redesign is that it
  feels "inconsistent and maybe unintentional".
- **The cellar stays easy,** and second.
- **Mobile first.** Desktop gets its own layout where the form factor earns one.
- **The centre ＋ is the universal action:** "The thing that drives generally taking an action
  despite wherever you are in the app. I could see it being Rate Something as you outlined but
  maybe even wider". It is reachable from every screen, it leads with rating, and it is aware of
  where you are (on a place, on an item, in a cellar).

## The open problem: a drink in a glass

The moment that never worked:

> when you're at a bar and you're trying beers, there's kind of like no super good flow in the
> app today, given like if you take a picture of just a glass, because often you get served in
> glassware, not the bottle or can. There's no easy way to like get it in the app.

The owner wants it designed and is wary of how:

> the primary motivation behind the work on like menus was to be able to like snap a picture of
> like a menu of beers, or like the whiteboard, chalkboard, or TV screens with a list, and have
> that kind of like seed the app with what the place has. But again, I think there's just a lot
> of friction around that for the end user when you really think about it.

So this is settled by feel, not by argument: the direction round carries **several playable ways
through the bar moment**, each with its friction counted (taps, typing, waiting), for the owner
to try on the phone. Candidates:

1. **Type a few letters.** ＋ → "What are you having?" → pick from results, or keep what you
   typed as a new thing → score. No camera, no AI wait.
2. **The place already knows.** At a place someone has scanned before, ＋ offers its list first.
   Scanning is something one person does once, and everyone after them benefits.
3. **Rate now, name later.** ＋ → score and a photo of the glass → done. The name is filled in
   afterwards, by you or from the photo and the place.
4. **Snap the board.** Today's menu scan, with the wait and the matching it brings, kept as the
   way to seed a place, not as the way to rate one drink.

What already exists on the branch: a "Log a drink" sheet with a score, a note, a place, who you
were with and a photo of the glass, reachable from an item page when you own no bottle. What is
missing is the way in.
